Case Study

AI-Powered Churn Intelligence — From Cancellation Detection to Personalized Win-Back in Under 60 Seconds

A single builder designed and deployed an end-to-end churn intelligence system for a high-growth vertical SaaS company. The system detects every customer cancellation in real-time, extracts the reason using AI, classifies it into a standardized 12-category taxonomy, enriches it with billing data, logs it to a structured database, and automatically drafts a personalized win-back email with a tailored discount offer.

The Problem

The company had thousands of paying subscribers. Customers cancelled regularly — some for addressable reasons (cost, feature gaps, quality regression), some for non-addressable reasons (retirement, career change). But the company had no systematic way to detect, understand, or respond to churn.

Cancellations were invisible. When a customer cancelled, a notification posted to a Slack channel. No one systematically read every post. No one aggregated the reasons. Cancellations were noticed days later — if at all.

Reasons were unstructured. Customers provided a reason and optional comment when cancelling. These lived in Slack as raw text. No structured database, no taxonomy, no way to answer "what percentage of cancellations this month were cost-related?"

Win-back outreach was manual and slow. When someone noticed a cancellation worth pursuing: read the Slack post, look up the customer in CRM and billing, draft a personalized email, send it. 15–30 minutes per cancellation — by which time the win-back window had often closed.

No trend analysis possible. Leadership couldn't answer: "Top 3 cancellation reasons this quarter?" "Are cost complaints increasing?" "Do psychiatrists churn differently from primary care?" No database to query.

The Solution: A Three-Stage Pipeline

Stage 1: Detect & Notify

Trigger: stripe.subscription.canceled webhook

When a customer cancels, a structured Slack notification posts to #users-cancelling with:

  • Customer email and medical specialty
  • Plan type and pricing cadence (monthly/annual)
  • Number of licenses
  • Churn risk assessment
  • Selected cancellation reason (dropdown)
  • Verbatim free-text comment (customer's own words)

This notification is the source event that triggers both Stage 2 and Stage 3.

Stage 2: Classify & Store (The Churn Database)

Trigger: Slack event in #users-cancelling

Pass 1 — AI Extraction: An LLM reads the cancellation post and extracts user_email, selected_reason, and cancellation_response. If no reason AND no comment exist, the LLM returns "N/A" and the pipeline stops.

Pass 2 — AI Categorization: A second LLM pass classifies into a 12-category taxonomy:

  1. Temporary Unsubscription — "Taking a break, will be back"
  2. Customization/Formatting — "Templates don't match my workflow"
  3. Cost Concerns — "Too expensive"
  4. Approval & Legal Concerns — "Organization won't approve AI tools"
  5. Transcription Accuracy/Note Quality — "Notes aren't accurate enough"
  6. Competitor Comparison — "Switching to [competitor]"
  7. Personalization — "Doesn't adapt to my specialty"
  8. Personal Circumstance — "Retiring / changing careers"
  9. Technical & Performance Issues — "Crashes / too slow / bugs"
  10. Usage Frequency — "Don't see enough patients"
  11. No Time Saved — "Not faster than my current workflow"
  12. False Churn — Accidental cancellation or billing issue

Billing Enrichment: Queries Stripe by email → retrieves canceled_at timestamp. This is the authoritative Unsub Date (from Stripe, not the Slack post time).

Database Write (Notion): Searches the Churn Database for an existing record:

  • No record: Creates a new row with all fields
  • Existing record with blank comment: Updates — replaces N/A with verbatim text
  • Existing record with prior comment: Writes to "Follow Up Comment" field — preserving both

This create/update logic builds longitudinal history. One row per customer, enriched over time.

Stage 3: Draft & Flag (The Win-Back Email)

Trigger: Same Slack event (runs in parallel with Stage 2)

Identity Resolution: Email → Stripe customer → CRM contact (HubSpot).

AI Email Composition — three distinct paths:

Cost-related: Offer $49.50/month for 6 months. Tone: empathetic to budget, emphasize ROI. References upcoming improvements.

Other addressable reason: Offer 50% off for 3 months. Acknowledges their specific stated concern. References their verbatim comment.

Non-addressable (retirement, career change): Returns "N/A" — no email drafted. A discount is irrelevant to someone who's retiring.

Emails are saved as Gmail drafts (NOT auto-sent) for human review. Subject line, HTML body, recipient — all pre-filled. CRM contact flagged with churn_exit_interview_email_status = ready_to_send.

Why drafts, not auto-send: Win-back emails are high-stakes. A bad one burns the relationship permanently. The AI eliminates drafting time (15–30 min → instant). The human retains judgment.

Structured data (previously nonexistent): Every reason classified into 12-category taxonomy. Queryable: "top reasons this month," "cost churn trend," "cancellation rate by specialty," "false churn percentage." Longitudinal: customers who cancel, return, and cancel again have connected history.

Personalized outreach: Emails reference the customer's specific reason — not generic. Offers are reason-aware. Non-addressable cancellations intentionally skipped.

Human-in-the-loop: Drafts, not auto-sends — team reviews before sending. CRM flagging triggers exit interview workflow. AI handles speed; humans retain judgment.

Key Design Insights

  1. Reason-aware offers outperform generic discounts — A customer who left for cost gets a specific dollar amount ($49.50/month) — not a vague "discount." Three-way routing dramatically outperforms one-size-fits-all.
  2. Drafts, not auto-sends, is the right default — AI draft eliminates writing time. Human review preserves judgment. Speed of auto-send, quality of hand-crafted.
  3. The taxonomy enables questions you couldn't ask before — "34% cost concerns (up from 28% last quarter), 22% accuracy (down from 31%), 15% competitor (stable), 12% temporary (seasonal)."
  4. Stripe timestamps, not Slack timestamps — Unsub Date comes from Stripe's canceled_at, not the Slack notification time. For cohort analysis, this matters.
  5. Create/update logic builds longitudinal history — Captures the full customer journey across multiple cancellations.

Builder's Note

Churn is not an event — it's a signal. Every cancellation contains information: why the customer left, what they valued, what they didn't, and how addressable their reason is. The company was already generating this signal — customers told them why they were leaving. It was just being thrown away.

This system makes churn legible. Every cancellation becomes a structured data point for trend analysis, a personalized outreach opportunity that arrives while the window is still open, and a longitudinal record that reveals patterns invisible in snapshots.

The fastest win-back email can't save a customer who left for the right reasons. But a personalized, timely, reason-aware email — drafted by AI and reviewed by a human — catches the ones who left for reasons the company can actually address.
